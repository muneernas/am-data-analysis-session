Core ideaDemystified

What is data analysis — really?

It’s the disciplined habit of asking good questions of information, looking for patterns, and turning those patterns into decisions.

Question → Evidence → Pattern → Insight → Action
Not just “making charts” Not only for statisticians A teacher superpower

Before Power BIMindset first

Charts answer questions. Bad questions waste time.

Weak

  • ×“Show me all the scores.”
  • ×“Make a dashboard.”
  • ×“Is attendance good?”

Strong

  • ✓“Which grade × subject pairs dropped from Term 1 to Term 2?”
  • ✓“Which skills or topics proved hardest for the cohort?”
  • ✓“Where does low attendance predict low scores?”
Move from “Who failed?” → “Which skills / strands / questions were hardest?”

Step 1 · LookBeyond the class average

Class average 75% is a starting point — not a story

Distribution

Are scores clustered together, or split into two groups?

The highs

Who mastered it and needs enrichment — not more of the same?

The lows

Who needs immediate intervention — and on which skill?

Actionable data is timely, easy to understand, and immediately applicable
Professional cautionDon’t get fooled

Four traps that fool smart people

  • 1Average addiction — a 74% class average can hide a 48% subgroup.
  • 2Correlation ≠ cause — robotics kids score high; robotics may not be why.
  • 3Cherry-picking — one shiny chart that fits the story we wanted.
  • 4Missing context — a drop looks scary until you see the assessment got harder.
Always ask: “Compared to what — and for whom?”
Step 3 · Ask whyDiagnostic matrix

Diagnose the root cause

Instructional

Was the teaching method misaligned with the skill?

→ Adjust strategy, try a new medium

Design

Was the question ambiguous or the task design flawed?

→ Clarify assessment, adjust the rubric

Readiness

Did students lack prerequisite skills or prior knowledge?

→ Scaffold gaps before moving on

Step 4 · Take actionThree levers

Turn insights into teaching moves

Reteach

Whole-class skill gap → a 10-minute mini-lesson on one pattern of error.

Differentiate

Segmented understanding → flexible groups: support some, stretch others.

Adjust

Future planning → change unit design, pacing, or assessment structure.

Lab brief: Ahliyyah & Mutran

File: ahliyyah-mutran-learning.csv — scores across Math, Science, English for Terms 1 & 2, plus attendance, support, and activities.

D
Write a 2-2-2

Challenge 01 · Foundations

Where are we strong — and weak?

Create a bar chart of Average Score by Subject. Add a slicer for Term. Compare Term 1 vs Term 2.

⏱ ~12 minutes Visual: Clustered bar or column Bonus: split by GradeLevel
Success looks like

You can point to the highest and lowest subject–term combination and say why a teacher should care.

Challenge 02 · Relationships

Does showing up pay off?

Build a scatter chart: AttendancePct on X, Score on Y. Optional: color by LearningSupport or GradeLevel.

⏱ ~12 minutes Visual: Scatter chart Ask: any surprising outliers?
Success looks like

One sentence on the attendance–score relationship — and one student (or group) that breaks the pattern.

Challenge 03 · Impact

Who is growing?

Compare Term 1 vs Term 2 for students in Math Intervention or Reading Support versus peers with None.

⏱ ~15 minutes Visual: Clustered column / line Insight must include “so what”
Success looks like

A clear verdict: support seems to help / needs review — then name one reteach, differentiate, or adjust move.

Culture checkMake data safe

Navigate the barriers

Data overload

Too many reports, no focus.

→ Ask only: who needs what support, and how much?

Lack of training

Low confidence reading metrics.

→ Analyze together in PLCs / subject teams.

Culture of fear

Data feels punitive.

→ Reframe as shared problem-solving, not blame.
