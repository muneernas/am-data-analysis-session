Step 2 · DefinitionCore idea

Number → pattern → insight → move

A number

  • ×“Class average is 5.3.”
  • ×“Student 14 is low.”
  • ×“Unit 2 looks weaker.”

An insight

  • ✓“Organizing is the weak criterion for most of the class, even when Analysing is strong.”
  • ✓“Median is 4 while average is 5.4 — a few high scores are hiding the typical student.”
  • ✓“So this week we reteach Organizing with a shared planner, then reassess.”
Insight formula: Who + which criterion/unit + compared to what + so we will…
Step 2 · WhyPurpose

Data sharpens judgment — it does not replace it

  • 1See who is slipping before the next report.
  • 2Check whether reteaching actually helped.
  • 3Talk to HoDs and parents with evidence, not vibes.
  • 4Stop drowning in marksheets — start deciding.
Works in every subject

Math, Arabic, English, Science, PHE, Arts — the method is the same. Only the criteria names change (Knowing, Analysing, Communicating…).

Step 2 · MindsetWhen we look

Autopsy vs biopsy

Data as autopsy

  • ×Only after the unit is over
  • ×One overall average
  • ×Filed for reporting
  • ×Nothing changes in class

Data as biopsy

  • ✓During learning, while you can still help
  • ✓Criteria, units, groups
  • ✓Used as a diagnostic
  • ✓A reteach, regroup, or redesign this week

Step 3 · Look carefullyTwo numbers, not one

Average tells the mix. Median tells the typical student.

Average 5.4 · Median 4

A few 7s and 8s pulled the average up. Most of the class is still around 4. Plan for the middle, not the highlight reel.

Average 5.4 · Median 6

Most students are doing well; a small group is dragging the average down. Regroup for support — don’t reteach the whole class.

If average and median disagree, the class is uneven. That is the insight.
Step 3 · See the classHow to read a heatmap

Green = mastery. Gold = developing. Red = needs support.

Scan a row for a weak criterion. Scan a column for a student profile.

S1S2S3S4S5Avg
Knowing 6 7 5 6 7 6.2
Investigating 3 4 3 4 2 3.2
Communicating 5 6 5 6 5 5.4
Insight: Investigating is a class-wide gap. Reteach that skill — don’t only pull S5 aside.

Step 3 · ActTurn insight into teaching

Three moves after you name the pattern

Reteach

Whole-class gap on one criterion → short, focused mini-lesson.

Regroup

Uneven class (average ≠ median) → support some, stretch others.

Redesign

Same gap across units → change the task, rubric, or sequence next time.

Step 3 · OutputYour finish line

After every summative: the 2-2-2

2 Strengths

Where did the class do well — which criteria or units?

2 Gaps

Which patterns need attention this week?

2 Actions

Reteach, regroup, or redesign — named, timed, specific.

This is what you leave with. Not a folder of screenshots.

Step 3 · CautionFour traps

Four traps that fool smart teachers

  • 1Average addiction — 5.4 can hide a cluster at 3.
  • 2Treating “—” as zero — not assessed yet is not a fail.
  • 3Blaming one student — if the row is red for everyone, it’s the skill.
  • 4Missing context — a drop may mean a harder task, not worse teaching.
Always ask: compared to what — and for whom?

Which criterion is the class story?

Write one insight sentence

“Most students are weaker in ________ compared with ________, so this week I will ________.”

Lab · 8 minChallenge 2

Is the class even — or split?

Compare average and median. Then check highest vs lowest student averages.

Decide the move

If they match → whole-class reteach is fair. If they diverge → regroup. Name which students need the first check-in.
